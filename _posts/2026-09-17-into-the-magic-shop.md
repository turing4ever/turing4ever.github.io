[Into the Magic Shop](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/316170/into-the-magic-shop-by-james-doty-md/) is a memoir by neurosurgeon James R. Doty. At twelve, growing up in poverty with a troubled family, he wandered into a magic shop and met a woman named Ruth. She taught him exercises to calm his mind, open his heart, and imagine a different future.

Doty goes on to become a neurosurgeon and accumulate considerable wealth. But he also loses sight of Ruth's lesson about keeping his heart open. His story raises a question that is more interesting to me than how to become successful: what happens after you get what you wanted?

It's easy to imagine that the next achievement will finally settle everything. A better job, more money, a bigger house. But if we never examine why we want those things, we can spend years becoming very good at chasing them.

I think this is the strongest part of the book. Learning how to pursue a goal leaves another question unanswered: is it a goal worth pursuing?

Ruth's kindness is the part that gives the story its weight. A child walks into a shop, and an adult takes him seriously. She gives him time, attention, and a reason to believe his life could be different.

We like stories about people who make it through determination. But where does that determination come from? Sometimes someone else has to believe in you before you can do it yourself.

Calm your mind. Pay attention to what you want. Care about other people. None of this sounds particularly complicated on paper.

The difficulty is remembering it when ambition takes over, or when things fall apart. I find the book more useful as a reminder to practice these things than as a formula for success. Ruth's kindness is already valuable, even without the remarkable career that follows it.
